Reappraisal simulator: HB 920, the 20-mill floor, and the 2026 property tax laws

Move the sliders. Three rules apply in order. (1) HB 920: reduction factors lower the rate on voted operating levies so they raise the same dollars after a reappraisal. (2) HB 335, effective tax year 2026: the county budget commission also trims inside (unvoted) millage so its revenue grows no faster than three-year inflation; BBHCSD's inside millage is 4.69 mills. (3) The 20-mill floor (R.C. 319.301): the combined operating rate can't be reduced below 20 mills, and once a district is on the floor its collections rise with values, except that HB 186 (tax year 2025 on) caps that growth at three-year inflation and credits the excess to the taxpayer as an "Inflation Cap Credit." All of BBHCSD's operating levies are current-expense levies, so HB 129's emergency and substitute levy changes don't apply. Figures are before the non-business and owner-occupancy credits, which HB 186 is also changing from 2026.

The inflation figure is an adjustable assumption; the Ohio Department of Taxation certifies the actual deflator used for HB 335 and HB 186.

Ohio House Bill 920 (1976) Ohio tax law

When property values rise at a reappraisal, HB 920 lowers the effective rate on each voted levy so the district collects the same dollars it collected before. Schools get no windfall from rising home values. Because voted revenue stays flat while costs rise with inflation, districts return to voters periodically for new operating levies. Two limits: inside (unvoted) millage, 4.69 mills for BBHCSD, is not reduced by HB 920 but from tax year 2026 is trimmed under HB 335 so its revenue grows no faster than three-year inflation; and the 20-mill floor (R.C. 319.301) stops the reductions once a district's operating rate reaches 20 mills. BBHCSD is at — mills, — mills above the floor.

The 20-mill floor and the 2026 property tax laws Ohio tax law

A school district's effective operating rate can't be reduced below 20 mills. Districts on the floor see collections rise with property values, which is why many Ohio districts never need a levy and others always do. Four laws signed December 19, 2025 and effective March 20, 2026 changed the rules: HB 186 caps growth in floor collections at three-year inflation and credits the excess to taxpayers ("Inflation Cap Credit," tax year 2025 on), phases out the 10% non-business credit by 2029, and raises the owner-occupancy credit to 15.38% by 2029; HB 335 limits inside-millage revenue growth to inflation at each reappraisal; HB 129 counts existing emergency and substitute levies toward the floor and bars new ones (BBHCSD has none); and HB 309 lets county budget commissions reduce voted levies they find excessive, but never below 20 mills for a school district.

The five Cupp expenditure categories What each cost bucket means

DEW splits operating spending into five buckets using the Expenditure Flow Model, which maps each district's USAS function codes to a category. Capital outlay and debt are excluded, and each bucket is divided by year-end ADM. BBHCSD's FY25 figures per pupil, with the county average in parentheses:

Instruction (—; county —). DEW: "expenditures associated with teaching and student instruction, excluding central office expenses." In USAS terms the 1000-series functions: classroom teachers and aides, their benefits, textbooks, classroom supplies, instructional software and purchased instructional services, across regular, special, career-tech and other instruction.

Pupil support (—; county —). DEW: "non-instructional services to students, such as counseling and health services." Function 2100: school counselors, nurses, psychologists, speech and language therapists, attendance and social work.

Staff support (—; county —). DEW: "support services to school districts' staff." Function 2200: curriculum development, professional development, library and media services, and instructional technology support for teachers.

Administration (—; county —). DEW: "school operation related to administrative personnel." Functions 2300 through 2600 and 2900: the Board of Education, the superintendent's office, building principals and their office staff, the treasurer's office (fiscal services), business services and central support such as human resources and data processing.

Building operations (—; county —). DEW: "building maintenance, utilities, and operation." Function 2700: custodians, maintenance and grounds staff, utilities, building repairs, security and property insurance. Note that this is the cost of running buildings, not the cost of building them; construction and bond debt are outside the operating total.
